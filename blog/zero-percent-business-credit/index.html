0% APR Business Credit Cards: Terms, Fees & Payoff Planning

Published: April 1, 2026 · Updated: November 18, 2026 | By Swift Line Capital

A business credit card with an introductory 0% APR can spread the cost of eligible purchases across a defined promotional period. It is still debt, and the offer is only useful when its terms fit your purchase and repayment plan. Compare the issuer’s written agreement before applying.

0% introductory APR is different from deferred interest

A true introductory 0% APR offer sets the rate on covered balances at zero for the stated period. After it ends, the applicable rate generally applies to the remaining balance going forward. Deferred-interest promotions work differently: an unpaid promotional balance can trigger interest dating back to the purchase. Do not assume the two offers are interchangeable.

The CFPB explains this distinction for credit-card promotions. Read your business card’s own agreement for the controlling terms.

Check which transactions the promotion covers

A purchase promotion does not automatically cover balance transfers, cash advances or cash-like transactions. Read the APR and fee table for each transaction type. An introductory offer is also different from a normal billing-cycle grace period.

For example, Chase’s Ink Business Unlimited page describes a 12-month introductory purchase APR when checked November 18, 2026. That is an issuer example, not a Swift Line approval promise or a guarantee that the same offer will remain available. Check the current application disclosure.

Compare these terms before applying

  • The eligible transaction type and promotional end date.
  • The APR after the promotion and whether it varies.
  • Annual, transfer, advance and other applicable fees.
  • Minimum payment requirements and late-payment consequences.
  • Approved credit limit, personal guarantee requirements and the issuer’s reporting practices.
  • Whether the intended business expense can actually be paid by card.

Build a repayment plan around cash you can support

Use the planned purchase balance and the number of repayment months to estimate a target payment. As a simplified illustration, paying a $12,000 eligible balance over 12 equal payments requires $1,000 per month before fees or additional spending. This is arithmetic, not a card offer or a forecast of your business income.

Then test a slower collection month or a delayed project. A minimum payment may leave a substantial balance at the end of the promotion. Do not base the plan on a future balance transfer being available or another application being approved.

Compare a card with other funding structures

A card may fit a defined, card-payable purchase with a credible payoff schedule. A revolving business line of credit, equipment financing or a term loan may have a different repayment structure. Compare total charges, access to funds, security requirements and payment frequency for the same amount and use.
