Start with the expense and the repayment plan

Swift Line Capital lists business credit cards alongside term loans, lines of credit and real estate funding. A credit-card conversation should identify the amount, intended transaction, required date and how the balance will be repaid.

An introductory 0% purchase APR is a temporary rate for covered transactions. It does not mean every transaction is free, that approval is certain or that a particular credit limit will be available. The issuer’s agreement controls eligibility, fees, payments and the rate after the promotion.

What to compare in an offer

  • Which purchases or transfers qualify and when the promotion ends.
  • Annual fees and charges for transfers, advances or other transactions.
  • The post-promotion APR and minimum-payment requirements.
  • Credit limit, guarantees and application requirements.
  • A payoff schedule that does not rely on another approval.

0% APR versus deferred interest

A deferred-interest promotion can add interest from the purchase date if the balance is not paid as required. A true introductory 0% APR offer has a different structure. Read our guide to terms, fees and payoff planning, with issuer and CFPB source links.

Prepare a useful funding conversation

Bring the planned purchase amount, whether the supplier accepts cards, your timing and a realistic repayment schedule. Ask who makes the credit decision, what fees Swift Line or the provider charges, and when any application or credit inquiry would be authorized. Approval and access to funds depend on the provider and complete application.

Compare the alternatives

Review a business line of credit, term loan or equipment financing when the use of funds or repayment structure calls for a different product.
